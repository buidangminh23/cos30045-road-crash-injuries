Introduction

Road crashes are one of the leading causes of injury-related hospitalisation in Australia. Deaths on the road receive most of the attention, but far more people survive a crash with injuries that need hospital care. Between 2011 and 2021, hospitalisations from road traffic crashes rose from 34,033 to 39,505 a year, an increase of 16%.

This page uses the Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE) hospitalised injury data to show who is being hurt and how this has changed over time.

Who is being hurt on Australian roads?

Car drivers are the largest group, with 13,327 hospitalisations in 2021 (34% of the total). The fastest growth is among cyclists: their hospitalisations rose by 51% between 2011 and 2021, from 5,391 to 8,163. Over the same period, pedestrian hospitalisations fell by 15%. In 2020, when COVID-19 restrictions reduced travel, most groups fell, while cyclist hospitalisations rose by 14%.

These are counts of hospitalisations, not rates per person. Australia's population also grew between 2011 and 2021, so a higher count does not always mean a higher risk. The dashed lines mark events that affect the trend: from June 2017 New South Wales hospitals stopped counting patients treated only in an emergency department as admissions, which lowers counts from 2017 onwards, and COVID-19 restrictions reduced travel in 2020. Victoria made a similar change from July 2012.
