Q1 National trend

Are fewer people being hospitalised after road crashes?

Recorded hospitalisations rose from 34,033 to 39,505. The annual trend, monthly distribution and seasonal views put the April 2020 low in context.

Q2 Road users

Which road users are hospitalised, and which groups are growing?

Car occupants are the largest group. Compare the growth of each group in counts, shares and changes from a common starting point.

Q3 Severity

Which road users have the longest hospital stays?

Compare how often each road-user category is hospitalised with average length of stay. Length of stay is a proxy for injury severity.

Q4 Age and sex

How do age and recorded sex shape who is hurt?

Compare the numbers by age, then switch the measure to investigate the length of hospital stays.

Q5 States and territories

How have trends changed by state of residence?

Select a state to inspect its trend. Victoria and New South Wales have recording changes. First Nations counts are sensitive to changes in how Indigenous status was recorded.

About the data

The charts use aggregated BITRE hospitalisation counts for road traffic crashes. The counts describe the burden recorded by hospitals. Comparing risk would require population or exposure data. Bed days per case use the 2016–2021 period after a recording rule change.
